RIDECHECK BUYER INTELLIGENCE AUDIT
READ-ONLY — DO NOT MODIFY CODE

OBJECTIVE

Audit the current RideCheck product to determine whether the inspection workflow, report-generation system, and customer-facing website are aligned with RideCheck's core philosophy:

RIDECHECK COMPETES ON INTELLIGENCE, EVIDENCE, AND RELEVANCE — NOT INSPECTION QUANTITY.

RideCheck does NOT want to become another generic "150-point inspection," "200-point inspection," or checklist-volume service.

Do not recommend additional inspection items simply to increase the number of checks.

Every inspection step should materially contribute to helping the buyer understand the actual vehicle being considered.

Do not modify any files, migrations, schemas, UI, prompts, APIs, routes, or database objects during this audit.

==================================================
1. RIDECHECK PRODUCT PRINCIPLE
==================================================

Evaluate the existing system against this principle:

Every inspection activity should help answer one or more meaningful buyer questions:

1. Is there observable evidence of a significant mechanical or functional concern?

2. Is there diagnostic evidence the buyer should know about?

3. Is there observable structural, corrosion/rust, flood, prior-repair, accident-repair, mileage, VIN, or title-related information relevant to the buyer?

4. How did the vehicle actually operate during available functional testing and/or road testing?

5. What financial exposure might significant observed findings represent?

6. What important areas could NOT be inspected, tested, verified, or accessed?

7. What evidence supports the findings?

8. Are there vehicle-specific characteristics or concerns that deserve additional attention for this particular year/make/model/trim?

9. Does the final report clearly distinguish:
   - physical observations
   - diagnostic evidence
   - third-party/history information
   - AI interpretation/context
   - unavailable/unverified information?

RideCheck should provide information and evidence.

The BUYER makes the purchase decision.

RideCheck should not recommend buying, rejecting, avoiding, or walking away from a vehicle.
