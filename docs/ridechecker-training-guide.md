## 1. Welcome to RideCheck

Welcome to the RideCheck team. You are now part of a network of trained inspectors helping real people make one of the biggest purchases of their lives — a vehicle.

Used car buyers can't always see the car before they decide. They may be buying from another city. They may not know what to look for. That's where you come in.

**Your job is simple:** Show up, document what you see honestly, and let the system do the rest.

You don't need to be a mechanic. You don't need to make a judgment call. The RideCheck platform guides every step of the inspection, tells you exactly what to photograph, and delivers a professional report to the buyer automatically.

**What you are:**
- A trained field professional who documents vehicle condition objectively
- The buyer's eyes on the ground
- A key part of making used car transactions more transparent and trustworthy

**What you are not:**
- A mechanic providing a diagnosis
- An advisor telling buyers whether to purchase
- A negotiator on anyone's behalf

---

## 2. What a RideChecker Does

A RideChecker's job has four phases:

| Phase | What Happens |
|---|---|
| **Accept** | You receive a job offer, review the details, and accept within the time window |
| **Travel** | You navigate to the vehicle's location and mark yourself as arrived |
| **Inspect** | You follow the guided inspection wizard step by step |
| **Submit** | You review your work and submit — the report is generated automatically |

**Time commitment per job:** Typically 60–90 minutes on-site, plus travel.

**The platform does the heavy lifting.** You don't write the buyer's report — the system generates it from your photos, measurements, and notes. Your job is to be thorough and honest.

## 5. Understanding the Dashboard

Your dashboard is your home base. It has five tabs:

### Overview Tab
The first screen you see after logging in. Shows:
- **Stats at a glance** — total jobs completed, active jobs, and earnings snapshot
- **Action Required banner** — if you have any pending job offers, they appear here at the top with a live countdown timer. This is always the first thing to check.

### My Jobs Tab
All your assignments in one place, organized by status:

Your earnings dashboard:
- Summary cards showing total earned, pending payout, approved, and paid

### Availability Tab
A 14-day calendar where you set when you're available for jobs:
- Tap a date to add an availability window (e.g., 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM)

### Training Tab
Access to training materials, certification status, and future modules.

## 6. Availability Status

**Setting your availability correctly is critical.** Ops uses your calendar to determine whether to offer you assignments.

**How it works:**
1. Go to the **Availability** tab
2. Tap a date
3. Add your available time window for that day
4. Save — Ops can now offer you jobs on that day

**Rules:**
- Only set availability for times you can genuinely commit to
- If your plans change, update your availability before a job is offered
- Do NOT mark yourself available and then decline every offer — repeated declines affect your standing (see Section 17)

> 💡 **Field Tip:** Set your availability for the full week every Sunday evening. This gives Ops maximum lead time to route jobs your way.

## 7. How Assignments Work

When a buyer books an inspection, Ops reviews the order and assigns it to a qualified RideChecker based on:
- Your availability calendar
- Your proximity to the vehicle
- Your ranking tier
- Current job load

**Two ways assignments reach you:**

**1. Broadcast Offer** — Ops sends the job to several available RideCheckers simultaneously. The first to accept gets it. This is why acting quickly on the countdown timer matters.

**2. Direct Assignment** — Ops assigns the job directly to you. You'll still see it in your dashboard and need to confirm.

### The Offer Timer
When a new offer arrives, a live countdown timer appears at the top of your dashboard under "Action Required." This timer is typically 15–30 minutes. If it expires before you respond:
- The job is automatically re-offered to another RideChecker
- A missed timer counts as an implicit decline

## 11. The Guided Inspection Wizard

The inspection wizard is the core of the RideCheck experience. It guides you through every section of the inspection step by step, tells you exactly what to photograph, and saves your work automatically.

**Why the wizard exists:**
- Ensures every inspection covers the same areas consistently
- Prevents anything from being forgotten
- Creates a structured data record that the report system can use
- Protects you — if you follow the wizard, you've done your job

**How it works:**
- The wizard is divided into **16 steps**
- A progress bar at the top shows how far you've gone
- The step counter ("Step 3 of 16") keeps you oriented
- Required steps must be completed before you can submit
- Optional steps add value but won't block submission

**Required steps** (must complete):
1. VIN Photo
2. Odometer Photo
3. Engine Bay Photo
4. Undercarriage Photo
5. Exterior (written notes)
6. Interior (written notes)
7. Mechanical (written notes)
8. Test Drive (notes)
9. Final Notes / Concerns

**Optional but strongly encouraged:**
- Tire Tread measurements
- Brake observations
- OBD Scan data
- Title & History flags
- Road Test Module

> ⚠️ **You cannot submit until all required steps are complete.** The progress tracker shows a count like "7/9" — this is required sections done vs. total required.

### Auto-Save
The wizard saves your progress automatically to your phone after every change. If you close the app or lose signal, your work is preserved when you come back. You do not need to save manually.

### The Step Navigation Buttons
- **Next** — advances to the next step
- **Back** — returns to the previous step (you can go back at any time without losing work)
- Tapping any step's title in the progress bar also jumps directly to that step

## 12. Step-by-Step: Each Inspection Section

### Step 1 — Confirm Vehicle
The first screen after opening the wizard. Review the vehicle details shown and confirm this is the correct car before proceeding. Tap **Confirm & Begin**.

> ✅ **What good looks like:** You're standing next to the correct vehicle. Year, make, model, and color all match what's shown.

---

### Step 2 — VIN Photo
**What:** Photograph the VIN (Vehicle Identification Number) plate on the driver's door jamb — the part of the car frame that the door latches into when closed.

**How:**
1. Open the driver's door fully
2. Look at the door frame near the top — the VIN sticker is usually there
3. Take a photo close enough that all 17 characters are clearly readable
4. Avoid glare — angle the camera slightly to eliminate reflections

**Why it matters:** The VIN is the vehicle's unique identity. It appears in multiple places on the car and must match. The system uses this to verify the vehicle's identity throughout the report.

> 📸 **Photo standard:** All 17 characters must be legible without zooming in on the final photo. If you can't read all characters, retake it.

> ✅ **What good looks like:** A clear, in-focus photo of the full VIN sticker with no glare, no fingers in frame, and all characters visible.

---

### Step 3 — Odometer
**What:** Photograph the dashboard odometer reading with the ignition on.

**How:**
1. Sit in the driver's seat
2. Turn the key to the "on" position (engine does NOT need to be running — just turn to the second click)
3. Photograph the full gauge cluster — show the entire instrument panel, not just a close-up of the number
4. The mileage number and any active warning lights should both be visible

**Why it matters:** The odometer reading goes directly into the buyer's report. The photo proves the mileage at the time of inspection. Any warning lights visible in the photo are also documented.

> 📸 **Photo standard:** The mileage number must be clearly legible. The full gauge cluster should be in frame. Warning lights must be visible if any are on.

> ⚠️ **If warning lights are on:** Note them — you'll document them in the Mechanical section too.

---

### Step 4 — Engine Bay
**What:** Photograph the engine compartment with the hood fully open.

**How:**
1. Pop the hood and prop it fully open
2. Stand above the engine bay — find a stable position to shoot downward
3. Take an overhead shot that shows the full engine area: oil cap, coolant reservoir, brake fluid reservoir, battery, and as much of the block as possible
4. Look for visible leaks, corrosion, damaged hoses, or aftermarket modifications

**Notes to add:** After uploading the photo, write brief notes on anything you observe — fluid levels, leaks, corrosion, unusual modifications.

> 📸 **Photo standard:** The entire engine bay should be visible in one frame. All four corners of the compartment should appear in the shot.

> ✅ **What good looks like:** A top-down photo showing the full engine, clearly lit, with no major obstructions. Notes mention fluid condition and any visible concerns.

> 💡 **Field Tip:** Use your phone's flashlight or a separate flashlight to illuminate darker areas of the engine bay, especially around the lower block.

---

### Step 5 — Undercarriage
**What:** Photograph underneath the vehicle — the frame, exhaust, suspension, and underfloor.

**How:**
1. Crouch or kneel beside the vehicle (use your mat if you have one)
2. Hold your phone flat against the ground or angled upward underneath the car
3. Use your flashlight — undercarriages are dark and details matter
4. Take multiple angles: front undercarriage, middle/frame, and rear near the exhaust and muffler
5. Upload the best wide shot showing the most coverage

**What to look for and note:**
- Surface rust (light surface rust is common and expected)
- Heavy rust, flaking, or structural rust on frame rails (note this)
- Exhaust condition — hangers attached, no holes in exhaust
- Fluid drips from above
- Any prior patch welds or repair work

> 📸 **Photo standard:** The frame rails and undercarriage floor should be visible. Photo should be in-focus. Use the flashlight.

> 💡 **Field Tip:** Turn on your phone's flashlight first, then position it. Don't try to aim the flashlight and take the photo simultaneously.

---

### Step 6 — Tire Tread
**What:** Measure or estimate tire tread depth on all four tires and document condition.

**How (with a tread depth gauge):**
1. Press the gauge into the main tread groove
2. Read the measurement in mm
3. Enter each tire's reading in the app: front-left, front-right, rear-left, rear-right

**How (without a gauge — visual estimate):**
- Good tread: Plenty of tread, wear indicators not reached
- Fair tread: Getting low but still legal
- Poor/Replace: At or near wear bars (approximately 2/32" or 1.6mm)

**Also check and note:**
- Uneven wear patterns (could indicate alignment or suspension issues)
- Sidewall cracking or bubbles
- Mismatched tire brands

> ✅ **This step is optional** — but always try to record tread depth. It's one of the most useful pieces of information for buyers.

---

### Step 7 — Brakes
**What:** Document brake condition based on what you can observe through the wheel spokes.

**How:**
1. Look through each wheel's spokes at the rotor and caliper
2. Observe rotor condition: smooth surface vs. heavy grooves/scoring vs. rust lip thickness
3. Observe pad thickness (visible through caliper)

**Notes to add:**
- Describe what you see on each corner: "Front rotors show light surface rust, pads appear adequate through wheel" or "Rear rotors show significant scoring grooves"
- Do NOT remove wheels or disassemble brakes

> ✅ **This step is optional** — but visual brake notes are always valuable. Document what you can see.

---

### Step 8 — OBD Scan
**What:** Plug your OBD-II scanner into the vehicle's diagnostic port and document any fault codes.

**How:**
1. Locate the OBD-II port — it's almost always under the dashboard on the driver's side, usually near the steering column
2. Plug in your Bluetooth OBD scanner
3. Pair it with your phone using the scanner's companion app (e.g., Torque, OBD Auto Doctor, or manufacturer app)
4. Run a full scan
5. In the RideCheck app, record:
   - Any Diagnostic Trouble Codes (DTCs) found — enter each code (e.g., "P0420") and its description
   - Emissions readiness status (pass/fail/incomplete)
   - Any warning lights active during the scan
6. Upload a screenshot of the scan results if your app supports it

**Common scenarios:**
- **No codes, all monitors complete:** Excellent — note this
- **Pending codes:** Codes present that haven't triggered the check engine light yet — note these
- **Active codes:** Check engine light is on and code is active — record all codes
- **Monitors incomplete:** Some emissions monitors haven't completed — this can indicate the codes were recently cleared; note it

> ✅ **This step is optional** — but OBD data is highly valued. Always plug in if you have the scanner.

> ⚠️ **If the seller refuses OBD access:** Document this in the notes. Write exactly what happened. This is NOT a failure on your part and will NOT hurt your score.

---

### Step 9 — Title & History Flags
**What:** Document observable indicators related to title condition, VIN verification, lien status, odometer disclosure, and visible flood, tampering, or prior-accident indicators.

**Important:** This step is **observational only.** You are documenting what you can physically see or verify on-site. You are NOT running a title database search, not certifying history, and not drawing legal conclusions.

**Sections in this step:**

**Title Review**
- Was the physical title available for you to view?
- If yes: What type of title did you observe? (clean, salvage, rebuilt, etc.)
- Did the VIN on the title match the vehicle VIN?
- Did the seller's name match the title?
- Was the title appropriately signed?

**VIN Verification**
- Verify the VIN on the dashboard (lower-left windshield) and door jamb
- Do the two VINs match each other?
- Photograph both VIN locations if possible

**Lien Status**
- Was a lien release document present?
- Is a lien noted with no release provided?

**Odometer Disclosure**
- Record the mileage shown on the odometer at the time of inspection
- Did the odometer disclosure on the title (if reviewed) appear consistent?
- Any physical signs of odometer tampering? (disturbed instrument cluster, inconsistent interior wear, replacement cluster indicator)

**Flood / Water Intrusion Indicators**
- Check for: water staining on carpet/upholstery, mold or musty odor, rust/corrosion inside cabin, mud or silt deposits, corroded wiring/connectors, moisture inside lights
- Select "None observed" if there are no indicators

**Theft / Tampering Indicators**
- Check for: ignition/steering column tampering, VIN plate appearance, door jamb sticker condition, key type, unusual aftermarket ignition wiring, lock cylinder damage
- Select "None observed" if there are no indicators

**Prior Accident / Repair Indicators**
- Check for: mismatched paint between panels, overspray on trim or seals, inconsistent panel gaps, replacement panels, body filler indicators, structural repair indicators, airbag cover replacement indicators
- Select "None observed" if there are no indicators

**Language to use:**
- ✅ "VIN mismatch observed" / "Indicators observed" / "Unable to verify"
- ❌ Never say "This car is stolen" / "This is a flood car" / "Title fraud"

> ✅ **This step is optional** — document what you can observe. Skip sections where information is simply unavailable.

---

### Step 10 — Exterior
**What:** Written documentation of the vehicle's exterior cosmetic condition.

**How:** Walk all the way around the vehicle and write detailed notes about everything you observe. Be specific.

**Cover:**
- Each panel: hood, roof, trunk lid, all four doors, front and rear bumpers, fenders, rocker panels
- Paint condition: chips, scratches, fading, peeling, mismatched color
- Dents or dings
- Glass condition: cracks, chips in windshield, any broken windows
- Trim condition: missing trim, damaged moldings
- Signs of prior bodywork: overspray on trim, uneven panel gaps, mismatched paint

> ✅ **What good looks like:** "Front bumper has a 3-inch horizontal scratch on the left side. Driver door shows two small door dings, approximately quarter-sized. Hood paint has light surface fading consistent with age. Windshield has a small chip lower-right corner. All other panels appear straight and consistent."

> ❌ **What poor looks like:** "Car looks okay." / "Some scratches."

---

### Step 11 — Interior
**What:** Written documentation of the vehicle's interior condition.

**Cover:**
- Seat condition on all seats: tears, stains, wear, burn holes
- Floor mats and carpet: stains, tears, unusual wear on driver's side
- Dashboard: cracks, broken trim pieces, sticky surfaces, warning lights
- Headliner: stains, sagging
- All controls: windows, locks, climate control, infotainment, horn
- Odors: smoke smell, mold/musty smell, air freshener masking something

> ✅ **What good looks like:** "Driver seat shows light bolster wear consistent with mileage. Passenger and rear seats have no visible damage. Floor mats are aftermarket, slight staining on driver's mat. Dashboard is intact with no cracks. Climate control functions normally. Light smoke odor detectable — not heavy."

---

### Step 12 — Mechanical
**What:** Written documentation of mechanical observations — things you can see, hear, or feel without disassembling anything.

**Cover:**
- Engine sounds during startup and idle: ticking, knocking, rattling
- Any fluid leaks observed (reference your engine bay photos)
- Transmission: smoothness shifting into drive/reverse
- Suspension: clunks, rattles when rocking the vehicle
- Steering: play in the wheel, pulling to one side
- AC / Heat: does it blow cold/hot?
- Lights: all exterior lights functioning (headlights, brake lights, turn signals, reverse lights)

> ✅ **What good looks like:** "Engine starts readily and idles smoothly. No unusual noises at idle. No visible fluid leaks observed during inspection. AC blows cold. All tested lights function. Slight play in steering wheel within normal range."

---

### Step 13 — Test Drive
**What:** Written notes from driving the vehicle.

**How:**
1. Ask the seller if a test drive is permitted (do not assume)
2. If permitted: take a short drive — ideally a loop that includes a stop, low-speed turning, and highway speed if safely accessible
3. Document what you notice during the drive

**Cover:**
- Acceleration: smooth, hesitation, surging?
- Braking: straight stop, pulling, pulsation/vibration?
- Transmission: clean shifts, slipping, rough engagement?
- Steering: tracking straight, pulling?
- Suspension: road feel, unusual bumps or clunks?
- Any warning lights that appeared during the drive?

> ⚠️ **If the seller refuses a test drive:** Document this. Write "Seller declined test drive request." This is NOT your fault and does NOT hurt your score.

> ⚠️ **If you are not comfortable with the drive for any reason:** Do not do it. Note why. Safety first.

---

### Step 14 — Final Notes / Concerns
**What:** Your overall observational summary and any specific concerns you want to flag for the Ops team.

**What to include:**
- Any items that stood out as requiring closer attention
- Anything the buyer's report should emphasize
- Items you were unable to inspect and why (access denied, time, equipment)
- Any seller behavior or situational notes relevant to the inspection context

> ✅ **This is your professional summary — be thorough.** This section is reviewed by Ops before the report is released.

---

### Step 15 — Road Test Module
**What:** A structured set of specific road test observations across multiple categories.

**Categories:**
- Acceleration behavior
- Braking performance
- Handling and steering
- Transmission behavior
- Noise/vibration/harshness observations
- Overall driving impression

Select from the options provided for each category. Add notes where you have specific observations to share.

---

### Step 16 — Review & Submit
**What:** Your final review screen before submitting.

**How:**
1. The app shows your completion status — which required sections are done and which are missing
2. Review the summary of what you've entered
3. If anything looks incomplete, tap **Back** and correct it
4. When you're satisfied, tap **Submit Inspection**

Once submitted:
- Your data is sent to Ops for review
- Report generation begins automatically
- You'll receive confirmation in the app

## 13. Concern, Critical & Not Accessible Statuses

Throughout the wizard, some sections let you flag the severity of what you found:

| Status | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Normal** | Standard finding — within expected range for a used vehicle |
| **Concern** | Something that should be investigated further — not necessarily a dealbreaker, but notable |
| **Critical** | A significant finding — may affect safety or value substantially |
| **Not Accessible** | You could not inspect this area (seller denied access, component was inaccessible, etc.) |

**Key principle:** Flag everything honestly. Do not downgrade a critical finding to "concern" to make the report look better. The buyer is relying on your accuracy.

**Safety flags and "not accessible" flags are never penalized.** If you document honestly, your score is protected regardless of what you find.

## 14. Using the Report Issue Button

If something unusual happens during the inspection — seller becomes hostile, vehicle doesn't match the listing, you can't access the vehicle at all, safety concern — use the **Report Issue** / **Message Ops** button.

**How to reach Ops from a job:**
1. Open the assignment card
2. Tap **Message Ops**
3. Describe the situation briefly and clearly

**Examples of when to message Ops:**
- "Seller is refusing to let me under the hood"
- "Vehicle is not at the listed address — no answer on phone"
- "VIN on the vehicle does not match what was booked — same model but different year"
- "Seller has become agitated — I'm leaving for my safety"

**Ops will respond and guide you.** You are not alone on jobs. The team is available during all inspection hours.

## 15. Auto-Save & Resume

The wizard saves your work automatically after every entry.

**What this means for you:**
- If your phone dies mid-inspection, all work up to that point is saved
- If you lose mobile signal, work saves locally and syncs when you reconnect
- If you accidentally close the app, reopen it and tap **Resume** — you'll be exactly where you left off
- You can leave the wizard and come back to it within the same job

**You never need to tap a "save" button.** Just keep moving through the steps.

> ⚠️ **One exception:** The draft only persists on your current device and browser. Don't switch devices mid-inspection.

## 16. Final Submission

When all required sections are complete, the **Submit Inspection** button becomes active on the Review screen.

**Before tapping submit:**
- Check that the required section count shows all required sections complete (e.g., "9/9")
- Review your photos — are they in focus and clearly show what they need to show?
- Review your notes — are they specific and detailed?

**After you tap Submit:**
- A confirmation appears
- Your inspection data is sent to Ops
- The report generation process begins automatically
- The job status changes to "Submitted" in your dashboard
- You're done

**How fast is the report generated?**
Typically within a few hours after Ops review. You do not need to do anything after submitting.

## 17. Understanding the RideCheck Score

Your RideCheck Score reflects the quality of your work and your reliability. It's calculated on each completed inspection and contributes to your overall performance standing.

The score is out of **100 points** across four categories:

### Checklist Score — 40 points
Whether all required fields were completed in the inspection. Completing all required sections earns full points.

### Photo Score — 20 points
- 15 points base: uploading all four required photos (VIN, odometer, engine, undercarriage)
- Up to 5 bonus points: additional photos beyond the required minimum

### Notes / Text Score — 20 points
Quality and detail of written notes across five key sections. Notes must meet a minimum length and specificity — vague one-liners earn fewer points than thorough descriptions.

### Timeliness Score — 20 points
- 20 points: on time
- Scales down proportionally if submitted late
- Less than 4 points if more than 24 hours late

### What does NOT hurt your score
- Documenting serious problems (critical findings)
- Documenting a seller refusal (properly noted)
- Marking something "Not Accessible" with an explanation
- A vehicle receiving a low score on the buyer's report

> Safety flags and honest documentation are always correct. You are never penalized for what you find.

### What does hurt your score
- Incomplete required sections
- Missing required photos
- Vague, one-word notes
- Late submissions
- No-shows after accepting a job
- Repeated declines (5 declines in 30 days generates a warning)

## 19. Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | What to Do Instead |
|---|---|
| Blurry or glare-affected VIN photo | Retake — angle the camera to reduce glare, move closer |
| Odometer photo with engine off (display dim) | Turn key to "on" position — engine doesn't need to run |
| Engine bay photo at an angle (can't see the whole bay) | Stand above, shoot straight down |
| Undercarriage photo in the dark with no light | Turn on your flashlight first, then position and shoot |
| Vague notes: "Looks fine" / "Good condition" | Be specific: panel by panel, part by part |
| Skipping the OBD scan because it seems complicated | Always try — plug in, run the scan, record what you see |
| Forgetting to mark "I've Arrived" before starting | Always update your status — Ops uses this to track jobs |
| Accepting a job you can't complete | Only accept if you're confident you can make it |
| Not messaging Ops when something goes wrong | Message Ops as soon as the issue arises — never wait |
| Telling the buyer what they should decide | Your job is to document, not advise |

## 21. Safety & Professional Conduct

### You Do Not Diagnose Vehicles
You are an inspector, not a certified mechanic. You document what you see. You do not:
- Tell a seller or buyer their car needs X repair
- Provide a professional mechanical opinion
- Certify that a vehicle is safe or unsafe to drive

### You Do Not Advise on Purchase
You never tell a buyer to buy or not buy a vehicle. You document conditions. The buyer makes their own decision with the information your inspection provides.

### You Do Not Negotiate
You have no role in price negotiation. If a seller or buyer brings up price, respond: *"That's outside my role — my job is just to document the vehicle's condition."*

### Seller Conflict
If a seller becomes uncooperative, aggressive, or refuses access:
1. Remain calm and professional — do not escalate
2. Say: *"I understand. I'll document what I was able to inspect."*
3. Message Ops immediately
4. If you feel unsafe at any point: leave. Your safety is always first.

### Report Unsafe Situations Immediately
If you observe something during an inspection that suggests an immediate safety risk to you (not about the vehicle's condition), leave and contact Ops.

### Professional Image
- Dress neatly — you represent RideCheck to sellers and neighborhoods
- Be punctual
- Do not smoke near the vehicle
- Do not eat or drink inside the vehicle
- Leave the vehicle exactly as you found it

## 23. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need to be a mechanic?**
No. The wizard guides you through everything. You need to know how to use a smartphone, plug in an OBD scanner, and follow instructions.

**Q: What if the seller refuses to let me inspect something?**
Document the refusal honestly. Write exactly what happened. This does not hurt your score. Ops reviews all inspections and understands that sellers sometimes restrict access.

**Q: What if I can't find the OBD port?**
It's almost always under the driver's side dashboard, near the steering column. If you truly can't find it after searching, note it in the OBD section and move on. Some older vehicles have the port in unusual locations — message Ops if you're stuck.

**Q: What if a warning light comes on during the test drive that wasn't on before?**
Note it in your test drive notes and in Final Notes. Photograph it if you can. This is exactly the kind of thing buyers need to know.

**Q: Can I take extra photos beyond what's required?**
Yes — extra photos earn bonus points toward your score and give buyers more context. More is always better than fewer.

**Q: What if the seller wants to read my report?**
Politely decline: *"The report goes directly to the buyer who commissioned it. I'm not able to share it."* Message Ops if the seller becomes insistent.

**Q: What if the seller tries to bribe me for a positive report?**
Decline professionally, complete the inspection honestly, and report this to Ops immediately. This is grounds for the seller's future bookings to be flagged.

**Q: How soon do I get paid after submitting?**
Payment is processed after Ops approves the inspection. You can view payout status in the Pay & Payouts tab. Approved payments are batched per your payout schedule.

**Q: What if my app freezes mid-inspection?**
Close the app and reopen it. Your draft is saved. Tap Resume on the assignment to pick up where you left off.

**Q: What if the car isn't at the listed address?**
Try calling the number Ops provided if available. If no answer, message Ops immediately with your situation. Do not wait around more than 15 minutes without Ops guidance.

## 28. Dashboard UX Improvement Notes

The following observations are provided for the product team to enhance the new RideChecker experience. These are not current functionality issues — they are enhancement opportunities.

1. **Onboarding first-run tour** — New RideCheckers see the dashboard cold with no guided walkthrough. A 3-step coach overlay on first login ("Here's your Action Required zone", "Here's your Availability calendar", "Here's where your jobs appear") would reduce early confusion.

2. **Equipment checklist pre-launch prompt** — Before a RideChecker taps "Start Inspection," a 5-second modal confirming "Do you have your OBD scanner and flashlight?" would reduce incomplete OBD sections.

3. **In-wizard photo quality hint** — After a photo uploads, a brief AI-check for blur or dark exposure could prompt "This photo may be too dark — retake?" before they move to the next step.

4. **Ops response time visibility** — When a RideChecker messages Ops from a job, showing "Ops typically responds in ~X minutes" would reduce anxiety during active jobs.

5. **Score breakdown post-submission** — After submitting, showing the RideChecker a preview of their likely score breakdown (sections complete, photos uploaded, notes length) before Ops finalizes it would encourage quality improvements immediately.

6. **Training tab content** — The Training tab exists but has limited content for new users. Even basic PDF downloads or embedded videos would make it immediately useful rather than aspirational.

7. **Spanish language toggle** — The public-facing site has partial Spanish support. Extending the inspector portal to Spanish would significantly expand the qualified RideChecker pool.
